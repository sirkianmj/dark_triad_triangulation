\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} Since its introduction, the Dark Triad (Machiavellianism, psychopathy, narcissism) has raised the question of whether these traits are distinct or manifestations of a shared Dark Core. Prior work relied on linear CFA/OLS, which cannot detect nonlinearities. Using existing Dirty Dozen data from community ($N = 5{,}902$), student ($N = 2{,}071$), and representative ($N = 1{,}492$) samples, we applied the Dark Triad Triangulation (DT$^3$) framework across five layers: network psychometrics, supervised machine learning, counterfactual tipping, semantic embeddings, and robustness testing. A bifactor CFA (CFI = .965, RMSEA = .066) outperformed the correlated three-factor model (CFI = .941, RMSEA = .077), with subscale reliabilities $\omega_{HS} = .036/.450/.532$ for Machiavellianism/Psychopathy/Narcissism that were robust in ordering across three bifactor specifications. Latent correlations were $r = .62$, $.61$, and $.36$. The 12-item network yielded three communities; in the 82-item space all DTDD items merged into one Dark Core. Supervised predictors showed a shared-and-specific pattern (agreeableness universal, empathy shared by Machiavellianism/Psychopathy, extraversion shared by Machiavellianism/Narcissism, Neuroticism as the sole directional dissociation). CKA on trunk activations was significantly below a permutation null; CKA on full-test-set head predictions reproduced latent correlations. Multi-group CFA confirmed configural and metric invariance. Semantic clustering showed an encoder-dependent single-trait fracture (ARI range .167--.571). With aligned predictors, OLS matched or marginally outperformed XGBoost, indicating that the explainable-ML contribution is interpretive rather than predictive. Findings support a strong general Dark factor, with Psychopathy and Narcissism retaining specific variance but Machiavellianism not.

\section{Introduction}

Since \citet{paulhus2002} first grouped together the three subclinical traits of Machiavellianism, psychopathy, and narcissism under the label ``Dark Triad,'' this framework has become one of the most widely used research constructs in personality, organizational, and social psychology. Much of its appeal derives from a foundational hypothesis: although the three traits correlate with one another, each represents a relatively distinct behavioral, motivational, and interpersonal pattern. Brief instruments such as the 12-item Dirty Dozen (DTDD; \citealp{jonason2010}) and the Short Dark Triad (SD3; \citealp{jones2014}) enabled this research to be conducted at scale in non-clinical populations.

Meta-analytic work has repeatedly questioned the distinctiveness of these traits, with evidence of substantial redundancy between the Dark Triad and the Five-Factor Model \citep{oboyle2015}, pronounced overlap between Machiavellianism and psychopathy \citep{muris2017}, and heterogeneous empathy profiles across the three traits \citep{shukla2025}. The DTDD's factor structure has been the subject of recent meta-analytic SEM investigation \citep{knitter2025}, and machine-learning prediction of Dark Triad traits has gained considerable traction \citep{leberecht2026}.

From the outset, a serious controversy emerged regarding the true independence of these traits. A meta-analysis by \citet{vize2018} showed that although the nomological networks of the three traits differ, these differences are often small and situated at the margins of a larger shared core; the Machiavellianism--psychopathy overlap in particular is so pronounced that \citet{miller2017} explicitly asked whether it constitutes ``a distinction without a difference.'' In response, \citet{moshagen2018} proposed the ``Dark Factor of Personality'' (D): a basic tendency to maximize one's own utility at others' expense, accompanied by corresponding justifying beliefs. From this perspective, Machiavellianism, psychopathy, narcissism, and related dark traits are surface manifestations of a single underlying disposition rather than fully independent constructs.

Empirical work on this controversy has relied almost entirely on classical linear methods---CFA and OLS regression. These methods have two important limitations. First, both assume linear, additive relationships and are thus ill-suited to detecting thresholds, higher-order interactions, or nonlinear functional forms. Second, latent factor correlations---typically used to adjudicate distinctiveness---are a summary, unidimensional index that cannot reveal whether the predictive pattern, internal representation, or semantic structure of a trait is genuinely distinct from that of its neighbors.

This motivates a testable middle-ground hypothesis: Machiavellianism, psychopathy, and narcissism share a meaningful common component (consistent with Dark Core theory; \citealp{moshagen2018}) while retaining distinctive patterns across psychometric structure, relational networks, predictive drivers, feature importance, and computational representations.

\subsection{Aims and Research Questions}

This study addresses three questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
\item When examined with supervised and unsupervised methods beyond linear factor analysis, do the three traits exhibit distinctive predictive patterns, internal representations, and semantic structures?
\item How does this distinctiveness change when the variable space expands from the 12 DTDD items to the full personality space (Big Five, empathy)?
\item Which trait, if any, shows the greatest functional overlap with another, and with which personality constructs is this overlap associated?
\end{enumerate}

The primary innovation is the integration of five independent computational layers within a single, reproducible DT$^3$ framework, enabling direct comparison across methods without sample or tool confounds.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Participants and Samples}

Data were drawn from the Czech validation study of the DTDD \citep{zivna2025}, with the underlying project materials and de-identified data available at the associated OSF repository \citep{zivna2023osf}. Raw files were reprocessed to ensure feature alignment across analysis layers; reported sample sizes may therefore differ slightly from the original validation study due to inclusion/exclusion criteria. Unless otherwise stated, all psychometric indices reported in the present article (including the CFA fit statistics in Table~\ref{tab:cfa} and the reliability estimates in Table~\ref{tab:alpha}) were re-estimated on the present analytic subsamples rather than copied from the original validation study; small numerical differences from the published values are therefore expected.

The full OSF release contains 17,517 raw records; preprocessing (Section~2.3) retained 9,465 usable cases, distributed across three samples: a community sample ($N = 5{,}902$), a student sample ($N = 2{,}071$), and a national representative sample ($N = 1{,}492$). Case-wise exclusion for missing auxiliary data (particularly the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, RSES, essentially absent in the representative sample; exclusion threshold $>90\%$ missing) reduced analytic $N$s per analysis (e.g., Layer~5 cross-validation for the community sample: $N = 5{,}215$). An independent subsample of 61 matched participants (from 3,913 test--retest records) was used for ICC estimation.

The community sample served as the training set and the representative sample as a fully held-out test set for all supervised components (Layers~2, 3, 5); the representative sample was untouched during fitting, hyperparameter search, or feature selection, and used only once for final scoring. The student sample lacked auxiliary variables (Big Five, empathy, demographics) required for feature alignment and was excluded from predictive-modeling layers, retained only for analyses not requiring the full auxiliary set (e.g., reliability).

\subsection{Instruments}

The Dark Triad was measured with the Dirty Dozen \citep{jonason2010}, 12 items on a 7-point Likert scale, four items per trait: Machiavellianism (manipulativeness, deceitfulness, flattery, instrumental exploitation), psychopathy (lack of remorse, moral indifference, callousness, cynicism), and narcissism (need for admiration, attention-seeking, status-seeking, entitlement). The Big Five was assessed with the BFI, empathy with the Toronto Empathy Questionnaire (TEQ), and self-esteem with the RSES. The RSES was available in the community and student samples and was included in the baseline OLS; it was excluded from Layers~2--5 because it was essentially absent from the representative sample.

\subsection{Data Preprocessing}

Steps included: (a) restricting age to 18--100 years; (b) removing flagged ``speeders''/low-quality responders; (c) using a minimum item count for composite scores rather than zero-imputation; and (d) requiring $\approx 80\%$ minimum item completion for BFI/TEQ/RSES composites.

\subsection{Baseline Psychometrics}

All confirmatory factor analyses reported in Table~\ref{tab:cfa} were estimated with \texttt{semopy} (Python 3.11). Internal consistency was estimated with Cronbach's $\alpha$ and McDonald's $\omega$. In the final pipeline, $\omega$ converged on the $\alpha$ estimate to within .02 in all nine subscale $\times$ sample cells (range: 0 to .017), consistent with the near-tau-equivalence of the four DTDD items per trait (see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Temporal stability used ICC(2,1) on the test--retest subsample; factor structure used CFA comparing one-, two-, and three-factor models, reporting $\chi^2$, df, CFI, TLI, and RMSEA. In addition to the three competing correlated-factor models (one-, two-, and three-factor), three bifactor specifications were estimated to guard against factor-collapse artifacts documented for short multi-facet scales \citep{knitter2025, eid2017}: (a) a symmetric orthogonal model in which one general Dark factor loads on all 12 items and three specific factors (Machiavellianism, Psychopathy, Narcissism) are fully orthogonal to each other and to $D$; (b) a bifactor-(S-1) model \citep{eid2017} in which Machiavellianism is designated the reference domain for $D$ (Machiavellianism items load only on $D$, and the two remaining specific factors $P_s$ and $N_s$ are allowed to correlate); and (c) a bifactor-(S$\cdot$I-1) model in which DTDD\_4m---the item that showed the largest general-factor saturation in preliminary analyses---is designated the reference item for $D$, freeing the three Machiavellianism items to load on a residual $M_s$ factor. The three specifications, taken together, provide a robustness check on whether the specific-factor structure is stable across reasonable identification strategies. Bifactor dimensionality indices---explained common variance (ECV), the reliability of the general factor ($\omega_H$), and the reliability of each specific factor given the general factor ($\omega_{HS,k}$, computed separately for Machiavellianism, Psychopathy, and Narcissism)---were computed following \citet{rodriguez2016}, with item uniqueness defined as $u_i^2 = 1 - \lambda_{Gi}^2 - \lambda_{Si}^2$ and total uniqueness entered in the denominator of $\omega_H$. Two theoretically motivated 2-factor specifications were also estimated: one merging Machiavellianism with Psychopathy (the \citealp{miller2017} redundancy hypothesis) and one merging Machiavellianism with Narcissism (the semantic-overlap hypothesis from Layer~4). Nomological networks were estimated via standardized OLS regression on the community sample using two predictor sets: (i) Big Five, empathy (TEQ), and self-esteem (RSES), the specification reported in Table~\ref{tab:ols}; and (ii) Big Five, empathy, and age---the aligned specification that matches the supervised ML layers (Layers~2, 3, 5) where RSES is unavailable in the representative sample. Specification (ii) is trained on the community sample ($N = 5{,}215$) and evaluated once on the held-out representative sample ($N = 1{,}492$) using the same train/test split as XGBoost, Elastic-Net, and Random Forest, so that the linear-vs-ML comparison is not confounded by predictor omission. Both OLS specifications are reported; specification (i) captures the fuller RSES-inclusive model, specification (ii) enables a clean cross-architecture comparison. All CFAs were estimated on continuous Pearson correlations using \texttt{semopy}. SRMR was not included in the reported fit statistics; ordinal WLSMV estimation with polychoric correlations is a further recommended sensitivity check that was not run in the present analysis.

\subsection{Computational Methodology: The \texorpdfstring{DT$^3$}{DT3} Triangulation Framework}

\subsubsection{Out-of-Sample Evaluation Strategy}

All supervised components (Layers~2, 3, 5) were trained exclusively on the community sample and evaluated once on the held-out representative sample; architectures, hyperparameters, and preprocessing were finalized via internal cross-validation within the community sample only. Network analyses (Layer~1) were conducted in Python 3.11 using \texttt{networkx} and \texttt{scikit-learn}'s \texttt{GraphicalLasso}, with an EBIC-style L1-penalty grid search (a Python reimplementation of the procedure described by \citealp{epskamp2018}); Louvain community detection used \texttt{python-louvain}. Supervised and semantic layers were implemented in Python (\texttt{xgboost}, \texttt{shap}, \texttt{scikit-learn}, \texttt{\seqsplit{sentence-transformers}}, \texttt{gplearn}).

\subsubsection{Layer 1: Unsupervised Structural Networks}
\label{sec:layer1_text}

Structural relationships were modeled via Graphical LASSO regularized with EBIC (\texttt{EBICglasso}; \citealp{epskamp2018}), with Louvain community detection \citep{blondel2008}. Two specifications were estimated: a DTDD-only network (the 12 core DTDD items) and a full-item network of 82 nodes comprising 44 BFI items, 12 DTDD items, 10 RSES items, and 16 TEQ items. An initial 88-node specification that included three experimental Czech-variant DTDD items (DTDD\_1g, DTDD\_1i, DTDD\_1ma) and three reverse-scored TEQ duplicates (TEQ\_CON\_2, TEQ\_CON\_4, TEQ\_CON\_14) was discarded after it introduced near-perfect linear collinearity and forced a pooled pairwise-deletion correlation matrix with $N_{\text{complete}} = 0$ requiring eigenvalue projection. The corrected 82-node specification restricts estimation to the community sample and uses only non-duplicate items; a within-community pairwise fallback (effective harmonic $N \approx 5{,}233$) is used where complete-case coverage is insufficient.

\subsubsection{Layer 2: Supervised Divergence and Representational Similarity}

Trait scores were modeled with Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost; \citealp{chen2016}), chosen for detecting nonlinear thresholds and interactions without OLS assumptions. Separate regressors per trait used an identical predictor set (age, Big Five, TEQ). A Shared-Trunk Multi-Task Neural Network (input $\rightarrow$ 128-d projection $\rightarrow$ two residual blocks $\rightarrow$ dropout $\rightarrow$ three output heads; Adam, lr $= 0.001$, weight decay $1\times10^{-4}$, batch size 128, 300 epochs, ReduceLROnPlateau) was trained in parallel. Representational geometry was compared via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA; \citealp{kornblith2019}) under three explicit specifications: (A) mean CKA between trunk activations of high-scorer row subsets, benchmarked against a label-permutation null; (B) mean CKA between 1D head-prediction vectors on high-scorer subsets (algebraically the squared Pearson correlation); and (C) mean CKA between 1D head-prediction vectors on the full held-out test set. Specifications (A) and (B/C) probe different quantities, and we report all three. Symbolic regression via evolutionary search (\texttt{gplearn}) additionally identified trait-specific functional forms without a priori shape constraints. As a cross-architecture reference, Random Forest and Elastic-Net regressors were also trained on the identical split (Rashomon Set analysis; see Layer~5).

\subsubsection{Layer 3: Counterfactual Tipping-Point Analysis}

A deterministic grid-search perturbation method \citep{wachter2017} quantified the minimal single-feature change required to shift an above-median case below the median, holding all other features constant. Age was held immutable and excluded from the search space. All continuous predictors were $z$-scored using the community-sample scaler, so a reported shift of $|z|$ denotes predictor SDs. Three metrics are reported: (i) percentage flipped, (ii) median $|z|$ shift conditional on flipping, and (iii) prediction-compression factor $\text{SD}(\hat{y}) / \text{SD}(y)$, which quantifies the variance-restriction artifact by which models with lower $R^2$ are easier to flip.

\subsubsection{Layer 4: Semantic Embeddings}

The original English DTDD item texts from \citet{jonason2010} were encoded with three sentence-transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, all-mpnet-base-v2, and BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5; \citealp{reimers2019}), followed in each case by hierarchical agglomerative clustering (cosine distance, average linkage; $k=3$ fixed a priori). Cluster assignments were compared to a priori trait labels via the Adjusted Rand Index (ARI), and per-trait cluster purity was computed. The baseline all-MiniLM-L6-v2 encoder was benchmarked against a 1,000-permutation null. Any structural claim about semantic clustering must be evaluated for encoder-invariance rather than inferred from a single model.

\subsubsection{Layer 5: Feature Importance and Robustness Testing}

Local/global feature importance used SHAP (TreeExplainer; \citealp{lundberg2017}). A SHAP Divergence Index (SDI)---a project-specific metric, mean pairwise cosine distance among mean absolute SHAP vectors---was benchmarked against a null built from 500 refits on randomly shuffled/recombined ``pseudo-traits'' derived from the same 12 items (limitation: not fully independent of the measurement system; Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Individual heterogeneity was assessed via Gaussian Mixture Models on local SHAP vectors (BIC-selected components). Architecture robustness was assessed via a Rashomon Set analysis \citep{fisher2019} comparing out-of-sample $R^2$ across XGBoost, Random Forest, and Elastic-Net on an identical split.

\subsubsection{Multi-group Measurement Invariance}

Configural, metric, and scalar measurement invariance across the three samples were tested for both the 3-factor and bifactor specifications using multi-group CFA with robust maximum likelihood (MLR) in R \texttt{lavaan} 0.7-3. Configural invariance holds when the same factor structure fits acceptably (CFI $\geq .90$, RMSEA $\leq .08$) in every sample; metric invariance holds when loadings are additionally constrained equal ($\Delta$CFI $< .01$); scalar invariance holds when intercepts are additionally constrained equal ($\Delta$CFI $< .01$).

\subsection{Ethical Considerations, Reproducibility, and Data/Code Availability}

This study is a secondary analysis of existing, de-identified data from the Czech validation study of the Dark Triad Dirty Dozen. The original human-subject research was conducted by the Olomouc University Social Health Institute (OUSH), Palacky University in Olomouc, Czech Republic. Adult participants provided informed consent, and the original study received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Theology of Palacky University in Olomouc (Approval No.\ 2020/4). The present authors were not involved in participant recruitment or the original data collection and did not conduct any new human-subject research.

Complete preprocessing and analysis code, validation materials, execution logs, and results tables are maintained in a public repository (\url{https://github.com/sirkianmj/dark_triad_triangulation}), with software versions documented through environment files (e.g., \texttt{environment\_dt3.yml} for the Python 3.11 pipeline). As the present analysis is exploratory and non-preregistered, the findings are hypothesis-generating.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Baseline Psychometrics}
\label{sec:baseline}

All baseline indices below were re-estimated on the present analytic subsamples (see Section~2.1); they therefore differ slightly from those reported in the original validation study \citep{zivna2025}. Internal consistency was acceptable for Machiavellianism ($\alpha = .802$--$.840$) and narcissism ($\alpha = .811$--$.846$) across all three samples, and marginal for psychopathy ($\alpha = .621$--$.763$), reaching its lowest value in the student sample ($\alpha = .621$, $\omega = .638$; Table~\ref{tab:alpha}). Temporal stability over the 61-pair retest subsample replicated this ordering: ICC(2,1) $= .826$, 95\% CI $[.726, .892]$ for Machiavellianism; $.836$ $[.741, .899]$ for narcissism; and $.637$ $[.464, .767]$ for psychopathy, all $p < .001$. The convergence of lower $\alpha$ and lower ICC for psychopathy is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} as a psychometric limitation of the DTDD psychopathy subscale.

The three-factor correlated model ($\chi^2 = 1838.65$, $df = 51$, CFI = .941, TLI = .923, RMSEA = .077) outperformed both 2-factor specifications: merging Machiavellianism with Psychopathy yielded $\chi^2 = 4589.6$, $df = 53$, CFI = .849, TLI = .812, RMSEA = .120; merging Machiavellianism with Narcissism yielded $\chi^2 = 6278.64$, $df = 53$, CFI = .793, TLI = .743, RMSEA = .141. Both merges fit substantially worse than the free three-factor model, and the Machiavellianism--Narcissism merge fits \textit{worse} than the Machiavellianism--Psychopathy merge despite the two trait pairs having nearly identical latent correlations ($r = .606$ vs.\ $.623$). This dissociation indicates that a latent correlation between two traits does not predict how much collapsing them into a single factor costs in model fit---a caution against using $r$ as the sole index of trait distinctiveness (Figure~\ref{fig:cfa_comparison}). Bifactor models with one general Dark factor and three specific factors fit better than the correlated three-factor model on every fit index. Under the symmetric orthogonal specification ($\chi^2(42) = 1109.75$, CFI = .965, TLI = .944, RMSEA = .066; Table~\ref{tab:cfa}, Table~\ref{tab:bifactor_loadings}), computation of the \citet{rodriguez2016} indices from the standardized loadings gives ECV = .576 and $\omega_H = .734$ for the general factor, and the three specific factors diverge sharply: $\omega_{HS,M} = .036$, $\omega_{HS,P} = .450$, and $\omega_{HS,N} = .532$. However, the symmetric specification forces four fully orthogonal factors, an identification strategy that is known to cause one structurally weaker facet to be absorbed as the reference domain for the general factor in short multi-facet scales \citep{knitter2025, eid2017}. To guard against this artifact, two reference-domain specifications were also estimated. In the bifactor-(S-1) model (Machiavellianism as the reference domain for $D$), the specific factors $P_s$ and $N_s$ are freed to correlate: $\omega_{HS,P} = .454$, $\omega_{HS,N} = .511$, $H_P = .632$, $H_N = .714$, $H_D = .885$, and the model fits acceptably (CFI = .959, TLI = .940, RMSEA = .068). In the bifactor-(S$\cdot$I-1) model (DTDD\_4m as reference item), the three Machiavellianism items load on a residual $M_s$ factor: $\omega_{HS,M} = .116$, $\omega_{HS,P} = .436$, $\omega_{HS,N} = .516$, $H_M = .255$, $H_P = .617$, $H_N = .719$, $H_D = .886$, with fit equivalent to the symmetric specification (CFI = .965, TLI = .944, RMSEA = .066). \textbf{Across all three specifications the ordering of specific-variance magnitudes is preserved: $\omega_{HS,M} \ll \omega_{HS,P} \approx \omega_{HS,N}$.} Following the \citet{rodriguez2016} criterion that $\omega_{HS} < .50$ indicates a subscale whose unit-weighted composite is dominated by general-factor and error variance, Machiavellianism falls clearly below the threshold in every specification examined, Psychopathy brackets it (.436--.454), and only Narcissism consistently clears it (.511--.532). The symmetric orthogonal specification's near-zero $\omega_{HS,M}$ (.036) is a well-documented artifact of the four-orthogonal-factor constraint, but its qualitative direction is confirmed by both reference-domain specifications; under the corrected bifactor-(S$\cdot$I-1) specification, Machiavellianism retains limited specific variance ($\omega_{HS,M} = .116$; $H_M = .255$) that is nonetheless far below the $.50$ (reliability) and $.70$ (construct replicability) thresholds. Machiavellianism is therefore best characterized as a near-pure indicator of the general Dark factor rather than as a specific dimension with substantial unique variance---a structural result that converges with its bridging position in the latent correlation matrix ($r = .623$ with Psychopathy, $r = .606$ with Narcissism) and with its intermediate role in Layers~1--5. Fit indices in the present analysis are from raw ML estimation; robust MLR estimation, as used by the original validation study \citep{zivna2025}, would inflate $\chi^2$ under non-normality.

\subsection{Layer 2: Supervised Divergence and Representational Similarity}
\label{sec:layer2}

Out-of-sample $R^2$ ranged from .132 to .218 (Table~\ref{tab:xgb}). The traits shared low agreeableness as a dominant predictor but diverged in secondary structure: empathy was specific to psychopathy, extraversion and openness to narcissism, and age to narcissism. SHAP interaction analysis found three distinct high-order interaction pairs; the single strongest interaction belonged to psychopathy (agreeableness $\times$ empathy; strength $= .186$). Method (B), 1D head-prediction CKA on high-scorer subsets, produced .0031---algebraically a squared Pearson correlation on restricted-range subsets, near zero because high-scorer predictions have little variance. Method (C), 1D head-prediction CKA on the full held-out test set, produced .4739---the true squared correlation between the three continuous prediction vectors, consistent with the traits' latent correlations ($r = .36$--$.62$). \subsection{Layer 4: Semantic Embeddings}
\label{sec:layer4}

Hierarchical clustering on item-text embeddings was evaluated across three encoders. \subsection{Layer 5: Feature Importance and Robustness Testing}
\label{sec:layer5}

The observed SHAP Divergence Index (SDI) was .2591, against a 500-iteration pseudo-trait null mean of .0953 ($SD = .0588$, $p = .0100$), exceeding chance-level partitioning. A signed directional SDI was also computed, weighting each predictor's mean absolute SHAP value by the sign of its association with the trait. The aggregate signed SDI (.287) did not reach significance against the signed pseudo-trait null (.137 $\pm$ .080, $p = .064$), because six of the seven predictors (Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Openness, Empathy, Age) predict all three traits in the same direction and dilute the one dissociating predictor. A per-predictor inspection is more informative: it identifies \textbf{Neuroticism as the single predictor whose directional effect separates Psychopathy from Narcissism} ($\beta = -.138$ for Psychopathy vs.\ $+.120$ for Narcissism; Table~\ref{tab:ols}), while the other six predictors share direction across the three traits. The unsigned SDI is therefore retained as the primary divisive metric, and the per-predictor signed comparison is reported as a supplementary directional analysis: the shared-direction predictors bind the traits together (shared-core hypothesis), and Neuroticism is the specific predictor separating Psychopathy from Narcissism at the feature-importance level (bifurcation model). The Rashomon Set analysis across XGBoost, Random Forest, and Elastic-Net showed a maximum $R^2$ variance of $\Delta = .019$, but per-trait values (Table~\ref{tab:rashomon}) reveal that Elastic-Net matched or exceeded XGBoost on psychopathy (.237 vs.\ .218) and narcissism (.144 vs.\ .132), while XGBoost led on Machiavellianism (.147 vs.\ .145). This indicates that the predictor--trait relationships are predominantly linear: the contribution of the supervised layer is interpretive (SHAP geometry) rather than predictive. GMM clustering of local SHAP vectors showed non-monotonic BIC curves (Figure~\ref{fig:gmm_bic}): BIC reaches its minimum at $k=4$ components for Machiavellianism and Psychopathy, and $k=5$ (boundary of tested range) for Narcissism. The shallow curvature after $k=2$ (e.g., Narcissism: 4097.6 at $k=1$, 3442.0 at $k=4$, 3373.0 at $k=5$) is weak evidence for continuous rather than categorical individual-level structure.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Central Finding: Distinctiveness Within a Shared Structure}

The five computational layers, together with baseline psychometrics, yield two seemingly contradictory but complementary findings. Every metric examining the three traits in isolation---the DTDD-only network, XGBoost predictor patterns, CKA representations, semantic embeddings, and the SDI---convergently showed distinguishable functional, predictive, and representational patterns. Every metric examining the traits within a broader nomological context---the full 82-item network and latent factor correlations---showed convergence into a shared higher-order structure. This mirrors the prediction of Dark Factor theory \citep{moshagen2018}: dark traits are surface, partly distinct manifestations of a shared fundamental disposition. The appropriate conclusion is neither ``three fully independent traits'' nor ``one unitary factor,'' but three statistically specified dimensions nested within a strong general Dark factor, with two of the three (Psychopathy and Narcissism) retaining substantive specific variance and the third (Machiavellianism) functioning as a near-pure indicator of the general factor---consistent with \citet{vize2018} and with the bifactor results reported in Section~\ref{sec:baseline}.

\subsection{Machiavellianism and Psychopathy: A Distinction Without a Difference?}

Machiavellianism repeatedly occupied an intermediate, bridging position: its highest latent correlation was with psychopathy ($r = .623$), and its counterfactual sensitivity to agreeableness was shared with narcissism---consistent with \citet{miller2017}. However, psychopathy's distinctive predictor (empathy, not agreeableness) across Layers~2--3, and its complete semantic separation in Layer~4, indicate that psychopathy retains a distinct functional/predictive identity despite high latent overlap with Machiavellianism, suggesting the overlap may be more a shared-latent-variance phenomenon than a predictive/semantic one.

\subsection{The Narcissism Fracture and the Centrality of Agreeableness}

The overlap between narcissism and Machiavellianism was independently replicated at both the semantic clustering level (Layer~4) and the counterfactual level (Layer~3), converging on agreeableness. This aligns with \citet{vize2020}, who identified low agreeableness as a leading candidate for the Dark Triad's shared core, contrasted with callousness/low empathy \citep{paulhus2014}, here specifically tied to psychopathy. This supports a possible two-branch structure: an ``instrumental disagreeableness'' pole (Machiavellianism, narcissism) and an ``affective callousness'' pole (psychopathy)---an interpretation requiring confirmatory testing. The latent CFA does not support the Narcissism Fracture as a covariance-structure phenomenon: the 2-factor model merging Machiavellianism with Narcissism ($\chi^2 = 6278.64$, $df = 53$, CFI = .793) fits substantially worse than the free three-factor model ($\chi^2 = 1838.65$, $df = 51$, CFI = .941). In fact, it fits worse than the 2-factor model merging Machiavellianism with Psychopathy (CFI = .849), despite the two trait pairs having nearly identical latent correlations ($r = .606$ vs.\ $.623$). The single-trait-fracture pattern (which trait fractures being encoder-dependent; Section~\ref{sec:layer4}) and the Neuroticism directional dissociation (Section~\ref{sec:layer5}) together support the bifurcation interpretation at a level of abstraction not tied to any one encoder or aggregate metric. The three-level dissociation (latent fit, semantic organization, feature importance) is itself a substantive finding, precisely the kind of dissociation that a single-level CFA or single-level network cannot reveal.

\subsection{Position Within the Existing Literature}

Using tools entirely independent of CFA, this study converges with the Dark Factor framework \citep{moshagen2018} that dark traits share an underlying disposition. The bifactor results reported here also extend the psychometric literature on DTDD factor structure: \citet{knitter2025} and \citet{eid2017} documented that symmetric orthogonal bifactor models systematically produce factor-collapse artifacts on structurally distinct facets, and specifically on the Dirty Dozen where the Machiavellianism facet is the weakest of the three. Our three-specification comparison directly replicates this phenomenon: the symmetric specification's $\omega_{HS,M}$ of .036 is an artifact of forced four-orthogonal-factor identification, and the bifactor-(S$\cdot$I-1) with DTDD\_4m as reference item raises $\omega_{HS,M}$ to .116. The substantive conclusion survives the correction---Machiavellianism retains limited specific variance across every specification, while Psychopathy and Narcissism consistently retain substantial specific variance---but the \textit{magnitude} of the collapse is specification-dependent. This convergence between our reference-domain re-analysis and the meta-analytic findings of \citet{knitter2025} constitutes independent verification of both literatures. Explainable-AI tools (SHAP, CKA) additionally reveal higher-order interactions, nonlinear functional forms, and representational geometry not accessible to linear regression or CFA alone. The Rashomon analysis, however, qualifies the claim that nonlinearity is empirically necessary: linear models performed comparably, and the value of the explainable-ML layer lies primarily in interpretability of predictor structure rather than in superior predictive power.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textit{Limited explanatory power of supervised models.} Out-of-sample $R^2$ (.13--.22) restricts inferences to nomological overlap rather than the full latent constructs.
\item \textit{Interdependence of the SDI criterion and null.} Both actual scores and pseudo-trait nulls derive from the same 12 items, so the SDI null is not fully independent of the measurement system and should not support causal/ontological claims.
\item \textit{Conceptual overlap between predictors and criteria.} Agreeableness and empathy conceptually overlap with the Dark Triad constructs themselves, so observed distinctiveness/overlap may partly reflect content overlap rather than an independent underlying structure.
\item \textit{Small semantic-analysis sample.} Layer~4 rests on only 12 item texts and three embedding models; further encoder families remain untested.
\item \textit{Non-causal counterfactual analysis.} Layer~3 identifies predictive-model leverage only, not causal relationships.
\item \textit{Symbolic regression complexity/overfitting risk.} Discovered equations are complex and treated as supplementary, not primary, evidence.
\item \textit{Student-sample constraints.} Missing auxiliary variables excluded the student sample from predictive modeling and cross-sample replication; replication claims are limited to the community--representative comparison.
\item \textit{Lower psychopathy-subscale reliability.} Lower internal consistency/stability for psychopathy (student-sample $\omega = .638$) may partly explain its apparent distinctiveness via measurement error rather than construct differences.
\item \textit{Cross-layer predictor mismatch for age.} Age was included in the supervised layers but not in the baseline OLS; the age-aligned OLS specification reported in Table~\ref{tab:ols_ml} resolves this at the linear level but not for the SHAP-based interpretation.
\item \textit{Exploratory, non-preregistered analysis.} Findings are hypothesis-generating and require confirmatory, preregistered replication.
\item \textit{CKA permutation null underspecified.} The permutation null used for the CKA analysis was constructed by re-running the network with permuted labels rather than by a fixed geometric reference; because the trunk is shared across the three task heads, the residual CKA between head activations depends on the exact layer at which representations are compared. The reported CKA value should therefore be read as a within-model contrast, not as an estimate of population representational divergence.
\item \textit{Counterfactual grid bounds and prediction-variance compression.} Two artifacts qualify the Layer~3 percentages: the grid search did not standardize perturbation bounds across traits; and the flip rate scales inversely with model $R^2$ (the trait with the lowest explained variance, Narcissism at $R^2 = .132$, shows the highest flip rate, 61.8\%). The standardized perturbation metrics in Table~\ref{tab:counterfactual} quantify this.
\item \textit{Signed SHAP and per-predictor directional dissociation.} The SHAP Divergence Index uses mean absolute SHAP values, which discards the sign of predictor effects. A signed directional SDI was computed (Section~\ref{sec:layer5}): it exceeded the unsigned metric in magnitude ($.287$ vs.\ $.259$) but the signed pseudo-trait null also inflated, yielding a non-significant aggregate result ($p = .064$). The aggregate metric conflates 6/7 predictor-direction agreement with 1/7 directional dissociation. A per-predictor signed comparison is more informative in this dataset and identifies Neuroticism as the specific feature separating Psychopathy from Narcissism.
\item \textit{Bifactor specification dependence.} The symmetric orthogonal bifactor model produces a near-total collapse of the Machiavellianism specific factor ($\omega_{HS,M} = .036$), a documented identification artifact on short multi-facet scales \citep{knitter2025, eid2017}. The two reference-domain specifications resolve this, yielding $\omega_{HS,M} = .116$ in the (S$\cdot$I-1) specification. The ordering of specific-variance magnitudes is preserved across all three specifications, but the \textit{magnitudes} are specification-dependent.
\item \textit{Partial measurement invariance.} Configural and metric invariance are supported across the three samples, but full scalar invariance is not ($\Delta$CFI $= .025$ for 3-factor, $.017$ for bifactor). Cross-sample mean comparisons on the DTDD should therefore be interpreted cautiously; the substantive conclusions do not depend on cross-sample mean equality.
\item \textit{Encoder-dependent semantic clustering.} Layer~4 tested three sentence encoders. Which trait fractures varies across encoders; ARI ranges from .167 to .571. The specific ``Narcissism Fracture'' label does not generalize and has been retired. Testing multilingual encoders on the Czech translations would be the appropriate next step.
\item \textit{$\gamma$-dependent network collapse.} The ``Dark Core Collapse'' in the 82-node network holds at the default Louvain resolution $\gamma = 1.0$ but fragments into two or three DTDD communities at other $\gamma$ values. The DTDD-internal cohesion signal is robust to specification, but the ``collapse into one community'' interpretation should be read as resolution-dependent.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Suggestions for Future Research}

Three directions are proposed: (1) preregistered, confirmatory re-testing of the encoder-dependent single-trait fracture and agreeableness/empathy centrality in independent samples; (2) robustness testing of Layer~4 across additional embedding model families and, where feasible, the 15-item DTDD variant with additional impulsivity/antisocial items, as well as multilingual encoders on the Czech translations; and (3) integration of the DT$^3$ framework with longitudinal designs to test temporal and individual-level stability of convergence/divergence patterns.

\section{Conclusion}

By integrating classical psychometrics with five independent layers of computational evidence---network psychometrics, supervised machine learning, counterfactual analysis, semantic embeddings, and cross-architecture robustness testing---this study provides convergent support for a middle-ground conclusion: Machiavellianism, psychopathy, and narcissism are three dimensions nested within a strong general Dark factor (ECV = .576, $\omega_H$ = .734), with Psychopathy and Narcissism retaining substantive specific variance ($\omega_{HS} = .436$--$.454$ and $.511$--$.532$ across three bifactor specifications) and Machiavellianism functioning as a near-pure indicator of the general factor ($\omega_{HS,M} = .036$--$.116$; mean signed specific loading $\approx .15$). The ordering of specific-variance magnitudes is preserved across the symmetric orthogonal, bifactor-(S-1), and bifactor-(S$\cdot$I-1) specifications, providing specification-robust evidence that Machiavellianism is coextensive with the general Dark factor while Psychopathy and Narcissism carry distinct trait-specific residuals. This distinctiveness is evident in predictive drivers, neural representations, and (in encoder-dependent form) semantic organization, alongside considerable overlap---particularly between Machiavellianism and narcissism around agreeableness. The semantic evidence takes the form of a stable ``one trait fractures per encoder'' pattern across three sentence encoders, rather than a stable Narcissism-specific fracture; the linear driver evidence shows a stable ``two shared-direction predictors per trait pair'' pattern (empathy for Machiavellianism--Psychopathy; extraversion for Machiavellianism--Narcissism) that is itself a strong confirmation of the bifurcation model. Given the limited explanatory power of the supervised models and the exploratory, non-preregistered nature of this analysis, the findings should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. Nonetheless, the convergence of several independent methods toward a single conclusion illustrates the value of integrative psychology--explainable-AI approaches for classic questions of personality structure.
